WELCOME TO THE CONTROL CENTRE

A calmer road starts upstream.

Vehicles travel upwards through fourteen gantries along 20 km of motorway. The full motorway and all fourteen gantries are visible together. Incidents have orange rings and labelled callouts between gantries. The overview keeps the road and gantries fixed. Vehicles are much smaller so the full 20 km can stay in view; Detail shows larger vehicle icons. Tap Detail, then choose a gantry or point on the road to inspect. Use Upstream/Downstream to move the close-up; Show all returns to the network. Tap an individual overhead sign to edit that lane directly on the road. Tap the gantry’s side label to set all three lanes. Tap elsewhere on the road to dismiss the controls. Changes apply immediately.

↖ / ↗ Merge: asks drivers to leave a lane when a safe gap is available. The arrow applies after its gantry; cars can continue in the lane while waiting. × Closed: vehicles merge or stop before the controlled section. Speed limits show mph.

For an incident, use a merge arrow at the gantry before it, then close the affected lane. Use the traffic desk to spot an approaching queue. Lower limits at the gantry before its tail to smooth arrivals, and watch hard braking, stopped vehicles and network flow. Suggested limits require your tap and never alter lane closures. Random incidents arrive at the traffic desk. Breakdowns always block the left lane; look for a stopped vehicle with flashing hazards. A recovery truck must reach each breakdown and load the vehicle before the lane clears. Circulating recovery patrols can attend nearby breakdowns; an entry-based truck is dispatched if none is available upstream. Recovery is automatic. Shed loads and pile-ups can block two lanes at once. Set arrows and closures upstream of both lanes; the crew clears the scene automatically. Debris and collisions take longer to clear. Reopen your signals once the obstruction is gone.

Flow counts vehicles leaving the motorway over the last simulated minute. Demand, headways, braking, lane changes, HGVs and imperfect compliance shape the result. Crowded traffic can use all three lanes even at lower limits; cars return left when there is room. This is an illustrative model, not an engineering prediction.

Police and ambulances target up to 110 and 100 mph respectively on clear roads; traffic yields when safe. Recovery uses the hard shoulder while attending a breakdown, then yields and rejoins normal traffic after loading.

Tap J1–J6 for slip-road controls. Roads are normally Open; Closed holds traffic for an emergency. Drivers match a moving gap to join lane 1. Motorway traffic may move right or ease off gently to help when safe. Demand surges arrive automatically. The notice above the road gives the roadworks location, gantry section and affected lanes. Prepare roadwork closures before crews enter, and reopen them after completion. Police rolling holds recur at varied locations, form a moving convoy and protect a clearance gap before releasing traffic. Network operations and ongoing performance monitoring are below the traffic desk.

Advance warning boards default to Automatic: queues, incidents, closed junction entries, wet roads and fog generate messages upstream. Open a gantry to set its warning board yourself. Messages encourage a gradual speed reduction; they do not close lanes. Demand shifts change automatically. The performance panel continuously tracks traffic served, accumulated delay, harsh braking, recovery times and a recent flow trend. Daylight, twilight, night, rain and fog cycle through the shift clock. Rain and fog build gradually, encouraging slower speeds and wider following gaps. At night, Detail shows white headlights and red rear lights; the overview keeps compact light marks so queues stay readable. Driver styles include cautious and unhurried drivers, commuters and loaded HGVs.

There is no losing. Pause, experiment, and find a rhythm.
